About / 01

Generation, interaction, and self-improvement.

My research focuses on multimodal generation and interaction, and on AI systems that can judge, verify, and continually improve.

I am especially interested in agentic real-time video generation and Code World Models: using executable code to represent scenes, objects, and events, and turning deviations in generated imagery into feedback that can be checked and localized. I also explore how infrastructure and harness design can support more natural real-time interaction.

For long-horizon agents, I study intermediate decisions and recursive self-improvement: how agents assess their state and direction, and when to continue, seek evidence, verify, change course, or stop. Building on CacheBridge, I also explore KV cache sharing across models in the same family with different parameter scales and post-training, its integration with agent harnesses, and how foundation models and agent-environment simulators can improve together on shared infrastructure.

I conduct long-term research on agent safety and long-horizon agents at the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences. During summer 2026, I worked on long-horizon agents and recursive self-improvement at LINs Lab, Westlake University. I also collaborated with Shanghai AI Laboratory in 2026 and currently collaborate with Vidu on research.

Attachment-Inspired Inherent Formation of Social Tendencies for LLM Agents

Explores attachment-inspired mechanisms for how LLM agents form and adapt social tendencies through interaction. The focus is on personalized soul agents: understanding the mechanisms behind stable social orientations, user-specific adaptation, and relationship development over time.

W.04
Under ReviewCo-first author (listed second)

Learning Which Evidence-Seeking Decisions Matter: Calibrated Counterfactual Replay for Language Agents

Final-answer rewards obscure which evidence-seeking decisions helped. PCCC-RL replays chosen and feasible alternative actions from the same saved tool state. It predicts credit for every action and uses inverse-probability residual correction on a randomized replay subset, directing a cost-aware budget to checks that most improve the training signal. History-only alternatives align these comparisons with the learning objective, yielding calibrated reinforcement-learning feedback for correct answers with complete evidence chains.
